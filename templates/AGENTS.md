# Academic standards for this folder

> Template shipped with [studykit](https://github.com/boa4ver/studykit).
> Copy it to the root of your notes folder and edit the bracketed parts.
> This file governs *principles* - how academic work has to be done here. It
> applies to every session in this folder, not only ones that invoke a skill.
> Operations - folder map, note format, what publishes - live in `CLAUDE.md`.
> Where the two disagree, `CLAUDE.md` wins on operations and this file wins on
> principles.

Every rule below is here because it went wrong for someone first. Keep the
reasons attached - a rule without its reason gets deleted by the next person who
finds it inconvenient.

## The teacher outranks the textbook

Where a teacher's own material conflicts with the textbook, the teacher wins.
Build study material from their outlines, slides, handouts, and scans first, and
from the textbook only to fill gaps. Their notation, rounding, and scope are
what gets marked.

## Complete the source packet before generating

Never generate from whichever file was easiest to open. Inventory every relevant
source for the active scope first, actually open them, and then list them.

Priority order: past papers and returned tests; the teacher's own slides,
outlines, rubrics and worked illustrations; the homework set and its answer key
when one exists; class notes; the textbook; prior mistakes.

**State the packet in the output every time.** A short Sources block near the
top of the artifact, repeated in the chat reply. Name what was *not* processed
just as explicitly. A polished partial reviewer is more dangerous than an openly
incomplete one, because it hides its own gaps.

A missing source is a normal state, not a blocker. Generate without it, say so
plainly, reconcile when it appears. Never fill a gap from memory while implying
the source was checked.

## Write to how you will actually be tested

Read the past papers and the stated scope before drafting, then say which
patterns you are writing to. The same correct content takes a different shape
for each teacher and each format.

Build every test from its own subject only - that subject's past papers,
materials, and results. Do not open another subject's practice test as a
template, not even for format. Borrowed shape is borrowed error.

## Worked examples must be exam-shaped, not answer-shaped

A worked example that announces its own treatment teaches nothing. "Less the
5,000 recovered from scrap" hands over the exact judgment the question exists to
test.

Give raw facts in neutral wording. Do not pre-sort the list. Make the decision
the visible work, line by line. Then name the trap.

## Teach the trap, not just the answer

Every practice item explains why each wrong option was tempting. The distractor
is the lesson. A key that gives only the letter has thrown away the part that
mattered.

## Contradictions are findings, not noise

When two sources conflict, never resolve it silently. Record both, recommend one
with reasoning, mark it unresolved, and say what would settle it. If one lesson
lists six elements and another lists seven, that discrepancy is a whole quiz
question.

## Label inference as inference

"The teacher said this" and "I concluded this" must never look alike. Mark claims
`stated | high | medium | speculation`. Anything about what will be tested is the
highest-risk category - a guess presented as fact sends you into a test prepared
for the wrong paper.

## Respect stated scope exclusions

If a teacher declares something out of scope, do not teach, drill, or include it.
Out-of-scope material is not harmless filler - it burns study hours that have a
deadline attached.

## Never fabricate the student's own experience

Opinions, reflections, anecdotes, and personal examples come from the student.
When a draft structurally needs one, it goes in as a loudly flagged placeholder,
repeated in the reply, never buried in the file.

## Date every external fact

Rates, deadlines, syllabus details, and news claims decay. Each carries the date
it was verified and its source, inline.

## Success criteria are scores, not feelings

- "review chapter 7" becomes "score 90%+ on a mock built from the past paper's pattern"
- "prepare for the essay" becomes "hit every row of the rubric"

## The work has to be the student's

Generated drafts exist to be understood, checked, and rewritten - not pasted.
Leave enough visible reasoning that the student can defend the work if asked
about it in class. Flag anything they could not explain in their own words.

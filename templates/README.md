# Templates

Two files to copy into the root of your own notes folder.

| File | Governs |
|---|---|
| `CLAUDE.md` | Operations - folder map, note format, what publishes, what to verify |
| `AGENTS.md` | Principles - how academic work has to be done here |

They are separate on purpose. Operations change when you reorganize; principles
change when something goes wrong. Mixing them means the principles get edited
every time you move a folder, and they stop being stable.

```bash
cp templates/CLAUDE.md ~/path/to/your/notes/
cp templates/AGENTS.md ~/path/to/your/notes/
```

Then fill in every `[bracketed]` part. A template left unedited is worse than no
template - it describes someone else's folders with total confidence.

`AGENTS.md` ends with an empty section for your own rules. Add one each time
something goes wrong, with the evidence attached. That section is what turns a
generic template into yours, and it is the reason the rules survive: a rule
without its reason gets deleted by the next person who finds it inconvenient.

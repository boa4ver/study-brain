# Reviewers, study notes, and cheat sheets

Read this before writing any note-shaped artifact.

## Opening block, in this order

1. **Title** - subject and scope, stated concretely. `CPAR Test Reviewer:
   Lessons 1 to 6`, not `Study Guide`.
2. **One subtitle line** - full subject name, instructor, test date/time/venue
   when genuinely known. `TBD` where it is not.
3. **How this may be tested** - a single ruled box carrying the teacher's own
   stated scope and format, and explicitly what is excluded. Attribute and date
   each line. Omit the box entirely rather than filling it with guesses.
4. **Sources** - small text, naming what was processed and what was not.
5. **Contents** - numbered, matching the note's main sections.

Do not invent a test date or format to fill the structure.

## Body

- Numbered top-level sections, each with a rule under the heading.
- Carry content in two- and three-column tables, tight bullets, and numbered
  lists. Bold the term, then a short gloss.
- Boxes are rare - reserved for the opening scope block and answer
  explanations. Do not wrap ordinary content in callouts.
- One idea per line. Long paragraphs do not survive a night before a test.

## Worked examples

Apply the exam-shaped rule from `SKILL.md` without exception. The short version:

**Wrong** - the treatment is announced, so no judgment is required:

> Cost of the machine 500,000; installation 20,000; **less** 5,000 recovered
> from scrap; annual insurance of 3,000 **which is not capitalized**.

**Right** - neutral facts, the decision is the work:

> The company paid 500,000 for the machine and 20,000 to a contractor who
> installed it. Scrap from the crating was sold for 5,000. An insurance policy
> covering the machine for its first year of operation cost 3,000.
>
> Then, line by line: why each item is in, out, or subtracted. Then the total.
> Then: *the trap here is the insurance - it is an operating cost of running the
> asset, not a cost of getting it ready to use.*

## Contrast pairs

Where two terms are confusable, put them in a table side by side with the single
distinguishing feature in bold. Most test items about near-synonyms are built on
exactly that distinction.

## Language

If the student sits a test in a language they read less fluently than they speak
the subject, gloss the technical stems. A student who knows the content but
cannot parse the question stem loses the same marks as one who never studied.
Put the gloss under the item, not in place of the original - the original is what
appears on the paper.

## What does not belong in a reviewer

- Material the teacher declared out of scope.
- Content copied from another course's reviewer for "format."
- Anything you could not point to a source for.

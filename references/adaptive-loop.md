# Deciding what to drill next

Practice without a record of what went wrong is just re-reading with extra
steps. This is the loop that makes the next set better than the last.

## The sequence

Within one assessment's scope, in order:

1. **Baseline test** - broad, balanced across the whole scope, in the real
   format. Its job is to find weaknesses, not to be passed. Everything after
   this is weighted by what it exposes.
2. **Targeted drills** - one weak subtopic each, short, repeatable.
3. **Weakness-focused mock** - still broad, but over-weighted toward the lowest
   subtopic.
4. **Final simulation** - full length, full timing, real format.

Do not skip to weakness-targeted practice when no baseline exists. Without one
you are guessing at the weakness, and a wrong guess costs a whole study session.

## What to record per attempt

Per item: final correctness, time taken, **which distractor was chosen**, wrong
attempts before the right one, whether a hint was used, whether it was flagged
for review. Tag every item with a stable subtopic so results aggregate.

The chosen distractor is the most informative field and the one most often
thrown away. *Which* wrong answer was picked says what the misunderstanding
actually is - the right answer's absence says almost nothing.

## Reading the record

- **Low accuracy, fast** - a real knowledge gap. Teach it again before drilling.
- **Low accuracy, slow** - the method is half-known. Drill worked examples.
- **High accuracy, slow** - it works but will not survive the time limit. Drill
  for speed.
- **One distractor pulling repeatedly** - a specific confusion, not general
  weakness. Build a contrast pair.

## When the scope is done

Keep the history, but stop letting it weight the current scope. Chapter 6's
results should not shape Chapter 7's recommendations. Archive the old scope's
analytics, exclude it from current calculations, and start the next scope with
its own baseline.

## Readiness

Recommend a final simulation only when every tracked subtopic has at least three
answered items and roughly 85% accuracy, and a baseline plus at least two
targeted sessions are done. Evidence below that stays a practice focus even when
overall accuracy looks fine - a high average usually means the strong topics are
carrying it.
